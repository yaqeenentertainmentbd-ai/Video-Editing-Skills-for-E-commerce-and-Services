## Music behaviour

- **Genre and palette:** clean motivational/corporate electronic bed with soft synth pulse, muted percussion and a polished tech-product feel. It avoids aggressive EDM drops.
- **Tempo:** approximately **104–108 BPM** in the main pulse. The edit often cuts on half-time accents, so it can feel closer to 52–54 BPM during speech.
- **Ducking:** the bed is consistently under the male voice, typically about **14–18 dB below dialogue**. It appears to duck a further **3–5 dB** during denser sentences, then opens slightly during full-screen graphics.
- **Intro versus body:** the first ~28 seconds use a brighter, more active arrangement with clearer pulses and more frequent accent hits. After the first chapter card, the body bed thins out and behaves more like a steady underscore.
- **Builds and dropouts:** short 1.5–2.2 s filter rises lead into the larger chapter cards around 00:28, 00:58 and 01:40. The music is briefly filtered or reduced at the impact, then returns under the next spoken line. The sampled body repeats this pattern around 11:13 and 14:16.
- **SFX relationship:** impacts sit above the music but generally remain below dialogue; small UI sounds are deliberately lower and often centered, while whooshes and risers use stereo width to stay perceptible without covering consonants.

## Patterns and practical takeaways

- **Almost always sounded:** full-screen blue graphic ↔ talking-head transitions; chapter titles; large proof/stat numbers; major phone/card landings; large before/after boards.
- **Usually sounded:** individual list diamonds, chips, app icons, price tags and the first item in a cascade. Subsequent cascade items often share one tick run rather than receiving separate large hits.
- **Usually left silent:** ordinary spoken-word caption changes, hand gestures, small reframes, static screenshots once established, and routine talking-head jump cuts without a graphic transition.
- **Layering rule:** major reveals commonly use a reverse or riser before the cut and a low impact at the landing. Small UI events get one short pop/click only.
- **Spacing:** the opening two minutes average roughly **20–23 discrete SFX events per minute**, with clusters during proof montages and chapter setups. The five sampled body windows average roughly **10–13 per minute**. Typical body spacing is **4–7 seconds**, while intro clusters can be **0.2–2 seconds** apart.
- **Hierarchy:** one dominant effect per beat. Secondary ticks/pops are 4–8 dB below the main whoosh or impact, keeping the voice clearly on top.
- **Tail discipline:** most UI sounds are dry and under 200 ms; whooshes usually finish within 350–700 ms; only chapter impacts, shimmers and risers carry obvious tails.
